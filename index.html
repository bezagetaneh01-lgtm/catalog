This catalog documents the gas leak data in PROD_GDM_PROD_DB across its three layers — Raw (as landed from Maximo and GIS), Curated (one row per leak, work order or reading) and GDM_RPT (the reporting layer). It describes structure: what each object contains, how objects join, and where each one came from. It holds no records.

How the objects connect

Almost every join uses one of two keys:

  • LEAK_ID — connects a leak (DT_LEAKS) to its readings, grade history, extent, status history, and work orders. In Raw it is Maximo's LOCATION.
  • WO_NUM — connects a work order (DT_LEAK_WORKORDERS) to its status history and its type-specific detail form. In Raw it is Maximo's WONUM.

See the for the Curated joins drawn as a map, and the for how each Curated object is built.

SELECT l.LEAK_ID, l.GRADE, l.STATUS, wo.WO_NUM, wo.WO_STATUS FROM PROD_GDM_PROD_DB.CURATED.DT_LEAKS l JOIN PROD_GDM_PROD_DB.CURATED.DT_LEAK_WORKORDERS wo ON wo.LEAK_ID = l.LEAK_ID WHERE l.STATUS <> 'CLOSED';

Querying in Snowflake — four things to know

  • Use fully qualified names — the same object name can exist in two schemas: PROD_GDM_PROD_DB.CURATED.DT_LEAKS (69 columns) and PROD_GDM_PROD_DB.GDM_RPT.DT_LEAKS (67 columns, the reporting copy) are different objects.
  • Quote column names that contain spaces or start with a digit. "BACKLOG YEAR" in VW_LEAKS_BACKLOG and "1 Quarter" in the PSC quarterly summary must be written in double quotes, exactly as-is, or the query errors.
  • Freshness is automatic. DT_ objects are Dynamic Tables that refresh when their target lag expires; views compute live. Check SOURCE_REFRESH_DT (Curated) or GDP_LAST_REFRESH_DT (GDM_RPT) before quoting a figure.
  • Raw is where the personal data lives. Addresses, coordinates, inspector names and free-text remarks sit in the Maximo and GIS landing tables; Curated carries some of them forward, GDM_RPT masks or omits them where a listing is shared. Check the column's classification before you copy it anywhere.

Want the live column dictionary for Curated? GDM_RPT columns are listed in full in this catalog; the Curated cards highlight key columns. Run this read-only query in Snowsight for every Curated column, type and comment:

SELECT TABLE_NAME, ORDINAL_POSITION, COLUMN_NAME, DATA_TYPE, IS_NULLABLE, COMMENT FROM PROD_GDM_PROD_DB.INFORMATION_SCHEMA.COLUMNS WHERE TABLE_SCHEMA = 'CURATED' ORDER BY TABLE_NAME, ORDINAL_POSITION;
Read-only metadata query — safe to run with any role that can see the schema. INFORMATION_SCHEMA shows only the objects your role is allowed to see; an empty result usually means a grant is missing, not that the objects don't exist.

The life of a leak Draft

Every Curated object maps to a stage. Select a stage to see which objects cover it.

In brief

A leak starts life as a location record in Maximo (the work-management system) and a set of work orders raised against it — an emergency odor investigation, surveillance visits, a repair, a recheck. Each visit produces readings and, over time, a grade history. GIS adds the point on the map.

Snowflake keeps three copies of that story, each shaped for a different reader:

  • Raw — the Maximo (MX) and GIS landing tables, exactly as the source systems hold them. Generic: a leak is one kind of location, a leak order is one kind of work order. Plus HIST_LMS, the frozen archive of the legacy Leak Management System.
  • Curated — 13 modelled objects, one row per business thing (leak, work order, reading, grading event, status change), named the way the business talks, auto-refreshing on a target lag.
  • GDM_RPT — 85 reporting objects: pass-through copies of the Curated hubs, the views behind each report and dashboard, and the lookup tables they share.

What is leak data Draft

Leak Data is the set of records generated and maintained across the full lifecycle of a gas leak — from initial discovery through classification, response, repair, and closure. It answers: where and when was a leak found, how severe is it, what work has been done about it, and what is its current status?

  • The leak itself — location, grade, status, cause
  • Field readings & grading history that justify its classification
  • Work orders raised against it — emergency response, monitoring, surveillance, repair — each a distinct order type with its own detail form
  • The audit trail of every status and grade change

HIST_LMS — the legacy archive raw · frozen

The pre-Maximo Leak Management System, lifted into Snowflake once (May 2025) with source column names intact and National Grid audit columns bolted on. It is not one system but three — one per legacy company lineage, with three different data designs. Nothing in Curated or the Leaks product reads it today; it exists for history and audit.

Things to know before querying HIST_LMS

  • Three designs. _LMS_NE is a proper relational database (LEAKS_LMS_NE is the header; class, status, repair and child orders hang off LEAK_NO); _LMS_NYC is a mainframe flat-file dump (every column TEXT, names with spaces and punctuation, filler columns); _LMS_LI is a DB2 extract with terse abbreviated names.
  • Quoted identifiers everywhere in NYC — column names with spaces or punctuation need double quotes, exact case and spacing. Mixed-case audit columns ("NG_LoadDate") must be quoted too.
  • Active and called-off leaks are separate tables in NE: a complete history is LEAKS ∪ CALLED_OFF_LEAKS.
  • History tables fan out exactly as in Curated — class, status and child-order tables are many rows per leak. Aggregate before joining to the header.
  • Keys do not always type-match (LEAK_NO is NUMBER in the header, TEXT in child orders) and many dates are stored as TEXT. Test a join's match rate before trusting it.
  • Machine-converted names. A find-and-replace rewrote INT→INTEGER inside column names (RPT_INTEGER_ST_NAME is the intersection street). Two test/backup tables (…_test, BACK_…) should be ignored.
  • Personal data — names, phone numbers, addresses, coordinates and free text — sits throughout; none of it is reproduced here.

What changes at each step

  • Raw → Curated — filter the generic Maximo tables down to leak work (class 1067, the leak order types), fold many work-order rows into one leak row, pivot attribute rows into columns, decode codes, pull in the GIS point, and give everything business names and proper types. The page walks through it, one object at a time.
  • Curated → GDM_RPT — reshape for one consumer at a time: a pass-through copy for the Marketplace listing, a page-shaped view per report section, a pre-computed Dynamic Table where a report is too heavy to run live (the PSC printout chain), and the lookups those views share.
  • Where GDM_RPT also reads Raw — some reporting views join MX directly rather than going through Curated; object-level lineage for those is being traced from their definitions.

Refresh and ownership

CURATED
Dynamic Tables with a target lag (4 hours on the leak hubs); refresh is automatic, not scheduled. SOURCE_REFRESH_DT carries the newest upstream change on each row.
GDM_RPT
Views compute live; the reporting Dynamic Tables carry their own target lag (1 day on the PSC printout, matching Curated's daily cadence). GDP_LAST_REFRESH_DT marks the load.
RAW
Loaded by the platform pipelines; NGLOADDATE / NGMODDATE are the audit stamps. HIST_LMS was loaded once and is not refreshed.
CHANGES
DDL on Curated objects goes through the platform developer; the product owner builds and tests changes in QA before they are promoted.

Reference

Naming conventions, company codes, leak grades, and work-order status vocabulary used throughout all three layers.

Naming convention

DT_Snowflake Dynamic Table — refreshes itself from its source query when its target lag expires. Used in both Curated and GDM_RPT (same name, different schema).
VW_View — computed live from underlying objects on every query
_DTSuffix used in GDM_RPT for a pre-computed reporting table (PSC_LEAK_PRINTOUT_DT)
WO_Work order — a work-order subject area in Curated object names
_LKPLookup / reference table in GDM_RPT (grades, statuses, jurisdictions, calendar)
RPT_Snapshot or listing table loaded by a GDM_RPT procedure (the NYC daily snapshots, the FERC listing)
_LMSVariant of a reporting view that reads legacy Leak Management System records
MX. / GIS.Raw landing schemas — Maximo tables and GIS layers, named as the source names them
NG_National Grid custom field or table in Maximo (NG_LKREADING, NG_WORKORDERSPEC, NG_LKGRADE)
NGLOADDATEPlatform audit stamp on every raw row — when the row was landed; NGMODDATE is the last modification
GDP_LAST_REFRESH_DTFreshness stamp on GDM_RPT objects — the last reporting-layer refresh

Leak grades (industry-standard convention)

Grade 1

Hazardous — requires immediate action.

Grade 2 / 2A

Potentially hazardous — scheduled repair or periodic monitoring within a defined window.

Grade 3

Non-hazardous at time of detection — re-evaluated on a monitoring cycle.

Exact repair/monitoring timelines are set by each jurisdiction's regulator and internal procedure — not shown in this data. The legacy NYC system also had a Type 4 (cast-iron encroachment), which has no Maximo grade.

Glossary — plain-English terms for newcomers

Grain
What one row in a table represents (one leak, one reading, one status change). Always check the grain before counting or joining.
Dynamic Table (DT_)
A Snowflake table that refreshes itself automatically from its source query — no manual loading. Its refresh frequency is called the "target lag."
Target lag
How stale a Dynamic Table is allowed to get before Snowflake refreshes it. It is the layer's service level — 4 hours on the Curated leak hubs, 1 day on the PSC printout.
View (VW_)
A computed result built from other objects on every query — e.g. an aggregation like the monthly backlog counts.
Raw / landing layer
The schemas where source-system data is copied as-is: MX (Maximo), GIS, HIST_LMS (legacy).
Curated layer
The modelled layer — one row per business thing, business names, proper types, deduplicated. Built from Raw by Dynamic Tables.
GDM_RPT
The reporting layer — objects shaped for a specific report, dashboard or listing, built on Curated (and in places directly on Raw).
classstructureid 1067
The Maximo classification code that marks a location record as a gas leak. It is the only thing that separates leaks from every other location in MX.LOCATION.
Quoted identifiers
A Snowflake rule: column names containing spaces or starting with a digit (like "BACKLOG YEAR" or "1 Quarter") must be wrapped in double quotes in SQL, exactly as written, or the query fails.
Leak grade
The severity classification of a leak: Grade 1 (hazardous, act immediately), Grade 2/2A (potentially hazardous, scheduled), Grade 3 (non-hazardous, monitored).
Work order (WO)
A dispatched job — an emergency response, a monitoring visit, a repair, or an inspection. Each has a type-specific detail table in Curated.
Recheck
A post-repair verification visit confirming the leak has cleared; surveillance is the scheduled re-read of an unrepaired leak.
Backlog
Open leaks that have not been repaired yet, tracked month over month by jurisdiction and grade.
PSC
The New York Public Service Commission — the regulator several reporting objects exist to file with.
SEI
A designation applied to Grade 3 leaks from their extent assessment (migration length, width, square feet), carried in the SEI reporting views.
LMS
The legacy Leak Management System(s) that Maximo replaced — three regional lineages (NE, NYC, LI), archived in HIST_LMS.
Jurisdiction / OPCO
The regulatory region (UNY, NYC, LI, MA) and the operating company code (5210–5330) that owns the asset. See the OPCO table above.
Maximo
The IBM work-management system that is the operational source of the leak and work-order data; MAXADMIN is its system user.
LEAK_ID / WO_NUM
The two join keys that connect nearly everything: leaks to their detail, and work orders to theirs. In Raw they are LOCATION and WONUM.
